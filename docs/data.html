Where the catalogue is thin (methodology, not just raster)

  • The database targets faults believed capable of M>6 in the Quaternary and is explicitly “substantially complete” but subject to revision (USGS faults page).
  • Nevada entries are commonly compiled from reconnaissance photogeologic mapping at 1:62.5k–1:250k with no trench or scarp-morphology studies — e.g. the Walker Lane entries noting "Trench investigations and detailed studies of scarp morphology have not been completed" (example Qfault report PDF). Desk-compiled segments, young cover masking scarps, and sparse fieldwork are exactly where extensions/splays go unmapped — the ground E1/E2 overweight.
  • INGENIOUS Quaternary-fault compilations (v1/v2 superseding) live on GDR 1391; slip/dilation-tendency grids exist for the same faults (doi:10.5066/P9YL58W6).

Shelved after verification

  • USGS Great Basin MT conductance maps (doi:10.5066/P9TWT2LU): five depth ranges spanning 2–200 km from ~800 stations — too coarse for 100 m traces. Regional prior at most.
