INGENIOUS and QFault: catalogue limitations

The USGS says QFault is a compilation of published maps and studies, used for seismic-hazard characterization; its traces and attributes are simplified geologic interpretations, and only a limited set of metadata fields has been maintained since 2017. QFault distinguishes evidence classes, including Class D nontectonic features such as erosional/fluvial scarps. Archived reports can preserve references and scale/reliability notes, but source scales differ. The Nevada Bureau of Mines and Geology separately cautions that some regional fault layers were digitized at 1:250,000 and can be spatially inaccurate at larger scales. Historical Nevada mapping also noted omitted smaller intrabasin faults; that is not proof of which targets the competition labels.

The INGENIOUS listing describes its Faults v1 as an updated compilation with attributes following QFault and its v2 as an updated compilation with field definitions. The v2 ZIP was not inspected in this sandbox after a prior download error, so no per-trace accuracy or field-validation claim is made here. Before any catalogue-derived analysis, inspect the actual source and whatever scale, location-certainty and evidence fields the downloaded archive supplies; account for location uncertainty; group related traces/source campaigns in spatial tests; do not treat unmapped ground as a verified negative or every line as field-confirmed. See USGS QFault methodology, historical Nevada catalogue gap, INGENIOUS listing, and the NBMG fault-layer scale warning ↗.

Data placement and verification sequence

  1. Retrieve competition files only through the authorized data tab; keep credentials private.
  2. Store private raw rasters outside Git; record SHA-256, source URL, retrieval time, license/terms, size, and contact in a local provenance manifest.
  3. Open every raster: verify CRS, band descriptions, dimensions, 100 m grid/transform, dtype, nodata and mask against the official sample.
  4. Compare any existing mirror copy byte-for-byte with the original official download. A manifest matching its own mirror only proves internal mirror consistency.
  5. Verify label semantics and scored footprint. Keep known training faults distinct from the test/holdout.
  6. Commit a source/split manifest and freeze whole-basin or whole-fault-system holdout blocks before implementing or tuning.
