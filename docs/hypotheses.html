Five new hypotheses (E1–E5)

Each names its layers, its physical signature, why it should catch faults missing from USGS/INGENIOUS, how it differs from everything already implemented across the group program (8GEMSDOE H1–H10 + H-A–H-E; 16GEMSDOE H16-1/H18-3a/H18-4; GEMSDOE4 Sato ridgeness; 6GEMSDOE magnetic HGB; GEMSDOE3 Pindrop; 10/12/15GEMSDOE scarp/ridge/alteration arms), at least one named non-fault confound with a distinguishing test, and a pre-registered falsification rule. None has been validated — validation is owed, starting with E1. Method citations are verified in References; data obtainability in Data.

E5 is a precision instrument whose direct DTI payoff is capped by α=0.2 but which earns its keep under exact top-k budgets by evicting systematic false positives.

E1 ★ — Euler-deconvolution depth-gated lineaments (VALIDATE FIRST)

Layers: reduced-to-pole magnetic anomaly, TMI horizontal + vertical gradients, isostatic gravity anomaly + slope — all competition bands (provided features).

Mechanism. A normal fault juxtaposes basement against basin fill (or offsets intra-basement units), creating a lateral contact in density and magnetization. Blakely & Simpson (1986) show horizontal-gradient-magnitude maxima sit over such boundaries, and Miller & Singh (1994) show the tilt-derivative zero contour tracks them independent of amplitude. E1 goes one level deeper: run Euler deconvolution (Thompson 1982; Reid et al. 1990) with a contact/step structural index (SI = 0 magnetic) along those edges. Genuine fault contacts yield tight clusters of solutions at the cover–basement depth; deep crustal fabric yields deep scattered solutions; survey noise yields unstable ones. Emission = density of well-clustered, shallow-contact Euler solutions, skeletonised to 1-px traces — a depth-gated lineament field, not an edge-amplitude field.

Why it catches catalogue-missing faults. The USGS Quaternary compilation maps faults with surface expression (scarps, offset layers — about page). Buried range-front faults under young cover have no scarp but still juxtapose dense, magnetic basement against light fill — the USGS Nevada gravity memo states isostatic patterns "reveal features of subsurface geology, including the location of buried faults" (USGS DS 234). Depth-gating is what separates them from the deep fabric that drowns amplitude-only edge maps.

Differs from implemented: 8GEMSDOE H1 (gravity TDR+HGM) and H7 (magnetic TDR×AS) emit edge amplitude with no depth discrimination; 6GEMSDOE's magnetic HGB and 10GEMSDOE's continuation arm likewise carry no source-depth estimate. No group line runs Euler deconvolution or gates on solution depth/clustering.

Named confounds + distinguishing tests:

  • Lithologic contacts (same SI, same edge). Test: solution-density contrast along known fault traces vs. SGMC-mapped lithologic contacts far from faults (SGMC: doi:10.5066/F7WH2N65); E1 must show excess density on faults after stratifying by contact length.
  • GeoDAWN Area 1 (200 m lines) vs Area 2 (400 m lines) seam — different sampling biases solution density (doi:10.5066/P93LGLVQ; "four blocks" is unverified — F09). Test: per-area normalisation + seam-exclusion rerun; the holdout verdict must survive dropping a 1 km band around the seam.

Pre-registration (locked before running): predicted direction — E1 top-k field beats budget-matched random AND the H1-style amplitude-only field on ≥2 of 3 leave-one-system-out folds in the buried-regime pseudo-truth, measured by the exact kernel DTI (scripts/dti_metric.py). Falsified if it merely re-ranks the same amplitude edges (rank correlation with HGM > 0.9 with no DTI gain). Full protocol: holdout protocol.

STATUS: UNVALIDATED — no data in this workspace; validation owed before any slot.

E2 — Riedel-splay fans from mapped tips, gated by strain-rate sign

Layers: catalogue fault geometry (labels) + dilatation rate, shear strain rate, second-invariant strain bands (competition).

Mechanism. The GeoDAWN area sits in/near the Walker Lane, a dextral transtensional shear system (Faulds & Henry 2008). Clay/sand shear experiments show strike-slip systems grow synthetic Riedel (R) shears at ~12–17° to the master zone (Tchalenko 1970; Naylor et al. 1986), plus extensional stepover splays where the corridor dilates. E2 projects single-tip fans at kinematically predicted splay angles (R-splay ≈ master strike ±15°, sense-consistent with dextral shear; stepover splays where dilatation > 0), each ray weighted by gap decay × angular match × mean positive dilatation along the ray — then skeletonised. It predicts where the rock mechanics says the next strand must go.

Why it catches catalogue-missing faults. Staff explicitly confirmed "new fault … can include newly mapped geometry of an existing fault system" (forum 11536), and desk-compiled catalogue segments systematically under-map short splays near tips and inside stepovers (cf. the thin-mapping discussion in Data § catalogue). This is the highest-value-per-pixel ground in the competition.

Differs from implemented: 8GEMSDOE H8 joined pairs of tips with straight corridors and was falsified on gap-bridging (native 0.0001 vs random 0.049) — E2 never connects tips; it projects single-tip fans at predicted angles with strain-sign gating, which H8 lacked. 16GEMSDOE H18-3a boosts near endpoints/junctions but with an isotropic complexity prior, not kinematic angles.

Named confounds + distinguishing tests:

  • Regional joint sets share orientations with R-splays. Test: fan orientations must associate with their parent master fault (angle + distance falloff from the tip), not with the regional joint rose; control fans projected at the mirror (kinematically forbidden) angle must score ≤ random.
  • H8's failure mode (corridors through empty ground). Test: leave-one-system-out folds with splay-regime pseudo-truth; pre-registered kill rule — falsified if E2 ≤ random on ≥2 of 3 folds.

Pre-registration: E2 beats budget-matched random and the mirror-angle control on ≥2 of 3 folds. STATUS: UNVALIDATED.

E3 — Fluvial SL-index / knickpoint-alignment detector

Layers: USGS 3DEP 1/3″ (~10 m) DEM + NHDPlus HR flowlines — both free public domain (3DEP listing; NHD access).

Mechanism. A fault with vertical throw crossing a drainage imprints a knickpoint (slope break) and a stream-length-gradient (SL) anomaly, SL = (dH/dL)·L (Hack 1973), on the channel profile; normalised steepness carries the same signal (Burbank & Anderson 2011, ch. on channel response to active faulting). Channels persist where scarps erode: a fault whose 2 m scarp has diffused below 100 m detectability still offsets every stream it crosses. Operator: route flow on the 10 m DEM (or adopt NHDPlus HR reaches), compute SL per reach, flag reach-scale outliers, then link outliers into traces only where ≥3 outliers from independent channels align within a 300 m corridor along a consistent strike (RANSAC line support). Emission = supported alignment segments.

Why it catches catalogue-missing faults. Compilers map offset layers and scarps; a fault system that offsets drainages without preserving a continuous scarp (degraded, aggraded, or plowed-out) leaves knickpoint alignments no scarp detector and no profile operator can see. The multi-channel alignment requirement is the catalogue-blind population's fingerprint.

Differs from implemented: no group line performs any fluvial-network analysis. 8GEMSDOE H3 samples an antisymmetric step along the gradient; H9 measures map-view orientation coherence; H-A runs hysteresis on curvature — all are hillslope operators. E3 is a channel-profile operator on routed networks.

Named confounds + distinguishing tests:

  • Lithologic knickpoints (hard/soft contacts). Test: alignments must cross ≥2 SGMC lithologic units; alignments confined to one mapped contact are discarded and the holdout rerun must survive the discard.
  • Dams, road crossings, canal drops. Test: NHD flowline attribution + TIGER roads intersection mask; alignments supported only by masked reaches are abstentions, not emissions.

Pre-registration: aligned-knickpoint segments beat budget-matched random and beat unaligned knickpoint density (the alignment requirement must carry the gain) on ≥2 of 3 folds. STATUS: UNVALIDATED. External data verified obtainable; ~10 m DEM for the footprint is a moderate download.

E4 — Paleo-geothermal + spring alignments intersected with geophysical edges

Layers: INGENIOUS paleo-geothermal features (sinter/tufa/travertine) + well/spring temperature & chemistry points — GDR submission 1391, doi:10.15121/1881483, CC-BY, small downloads (82 kB + 19.85 MB) — intersected with E1/HGM potential-field edges.

Mechanism. Fault damage zones are vertical fluid conduits: upwelling geothermal fluids precipitate sinter/tufa/travertine at the surface and feed hot springs along the trace, even where the scarp is erased and the magnetic contrast is weak. The physics (hydrothermal plumbing + surface precipitation) is mechanistically independent of potential-field edge physics (density/susceptibility contrast) — so a pixel where a deposit/spring alignment coincides with a gravity/magnetic edge is weighed as evidence in the proper sense: joint agreement of independent origins is far less likely by chance. Operator: kernel-density ridges + RANSAC line support on deposits/springs, emission = alignment pixels within 300 m of an E1 edge.

Why it catches catalogue-missing faults. Blind and degraded faults with active or recent fluid flow advertise themselves geochemically while showing compilers nothing mappable. The deposits are also exactly the "structures indicative of geothermal resources" the prize exists to find.

Differs from implemented: no group line uses point-feature alignments at all; 8GEMSDOE H-C intersects radiometric ratios with lineaments (different physics, no point alignments); nothing touches the GDR 1391 deposit/spring compilations.

Named confounds + distinguishing tests:

  • Valley-bottom springs / lithologic-contact seeps align with topography, not faults. Test: alignments parallel to valley axes or confined to one SGMC contact score lower than alignments cutting across them; the intersection with E1 edges must carry the gain (ablation: alignments alone vs. intersection).
  • Sparse points → spurious RANSAC lines. Test: pre-registered minimum support (≥4 independent deposits/springs per segment); permutation control (randomised point locations must yield ~zero supported segments).

Pre-registration: intersection beats alignments-alone and edges-alone (super-additivity of independent evidence) on ≥2 of 3 folds. STATUS: UNVALIDATED.

E5 — Scarp diffusion-age discriminator

Layers: 10 m DEM for inference; 1 m 3DEP tiles for calibration subset (competition 1m_DEM_links.csv; full-region ≈130 GB per sibling measurement — re-verify before budgeting — F10).

Mechanism. Scarp degradation obeys the linear diffusion equation ∂h/∂t = κ∂²h/∂x², whose solution is an error-function profile parameterised by diffusive age κt (Hanks et al. 1984; inverse solution for age: Andrews & Hanks 1985). E5 fits κt to every H3/H-A candidate scarp profile: fresh anthropogenic steps (road cuts, berms, κt ≈ 0) and over-mature shorelines (Pleistocene Lake Lahontan high-stand scarps, morphologically analysed in this very region by Hanks & Wallace 1985) are rejected; scarps with Quaternary-consistent κt are retained. Shorelines get a second kill-test for free: they parallel elevation contours, faults cut across them.

Why it matters for scoring. E5 is a precision instrument: with α=0.2 its direct DTI payoff is capped, but under exact top-k budgets every evicted berm/shoreline pixel is a recalled fault pixel elsewhere. It converts H3 from a step detector into a fault-step detector.

Differs from implemented: 8GEMSDOE H3/H-A detect steps; no line dates them or rejects anthropogenic/shoreline steps. No group line fits diffusion age or applies the contour-parallelism shoreline test.

Named confounds + distinguishing tests:

  • Composite (multi-event) scarps violate single-event κt. Test: two-event diffusion fit where single-event residuals fail; aspect-stratified κ (diffusivity is microclimatically controlled — retain only within-aspect comparisons).
  • Plowed/graded fault scarps look fresh (low κt) but are tectonic. Test: κt≈0 rejections must intersect the TIGER-roads / land-use mask to be discarded; low-κt steps far from any road are abstentions, not rejections.

Pre-registration: H3+E5 (dated) beats H3-alone at matched budget on ≥2 of 3 folds, with the gain attributable to FP eviction (measured FP-rate drop on road/shoreline masks). STATUS: UNVALIDATED — and the most expensive arm; run last.

What was deliberately NOT proposed

  • MT depth-slice conductance lineaments — investigated and shelved: the USGS Great Basin conductance maps start at 2 km depth and derive from ~800 stations over the whole Great Basin (doi:10.5066/P9TWT2LU) — far too coarse to resolve 100 m traces. Usable at most as a regional zone prior, not a detector.
  • Re-tuned blenders / generic ridge stacks — the group has run this family to diminishing returns (dense-ridge control 0.1152; HGB 0.0286; apex in-sample arms that collapse out-of-sample). New slots go only to new physics.
  • Tip-to-tip straight corridors — already falsified (H8: 0.0001 vs random 0.049). E2 is the kinematically constrained successor, not a retry.

Validation contract (applies to all five)

  1. Spatially blocked folds (leave-one-fault-system-out) with a 300 m train-exclusion buffer — no train pixel inside the metric kernel of test truth.
  2. Budget-matched random control + a mechanism-specific control (E1: amplitude-only edges; E2: mirror-angle fans; E3: unaligned knickpoints; E4: each arm alone; E5: undated steps).
  3. Hypothesis + predicted direction written before running (above); a further locked slice tested once, at the end; improvement counts only if it survives the locked slice and the investigator's best artifact argument. Full text: holdout protocol.
