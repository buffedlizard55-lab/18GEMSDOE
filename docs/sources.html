Official fault-catalogue and geology sources

S9 · USGS Quaternary Faults / QFault methodology

USGS Faults and QFault page ↗ · Archived record example (PDF) ↗

USGS describes an archive compiled from many published sources; its hazard traces and metadata are simplified geologic interpretations. The page notes limited maintained metadata since 2017 and distinguishes evidence classes, including nontectonic Class D examples such as erosional/fluvial scarps. Archived reports show source trails and, for some records, scale/location-reliability notes. This is a heterogeneous catalogue, not a uniformly complete or field-verified census.

S10 · Historical Nevada catalogue gap

NBMG, “Nevada’s Quaternary Fault Hazard” (1993) ↗

The first-cut statewide hazard data set focused on the largest faults and omitted numerous smaller intrabasin Quaternary faults. Historical evidence only—not a statement about current QFault completeness or competition labels.

S11 · Detailed Quaternary map-method example

USGS Las Vegas Valley surficial geology & fault map (2024) ↗

1:50,000 compilation integrates 1:24,000 maps, new field/desktop review, lidar in undeveloped areas, historic photos in urban areas and age constraints. An example of detailed work, not a regional quality guarantee.

S12 · INGENIOUS Great Basin compilation

DOE Geothermal Data Repository 1391 ↗

Public listing states CC BY 4.0; QFault-following v1 and an updated v2 compilation with field definitions. The v2 archive bytes/fields were not inspected in this sandbox because a prior direct download returned HTTP 500. No unverified per-trace accuracy or field-verification claim is made.

S13 · USGS western Great Basin paleolake GIS

MF-2323 map and downloads ↗ · Metadata ↗

Publicly listed GIS; shoreline positions were interpreted from 3-arc-second (~90 m) DEM contours, suitable for regional context, not fine 100 m offsets by itself.

S14 · USGS 3DEP one-meter DEMs

Data.gov collection record ↗ · USGS 3DEP ↗

Public-domain collection; products may be seamless within projects but not across projects. Exact study footprint and project seams are unverified.

S15 · GeoDAWN survey specifications

USGS GeoDAWN release ↗

Acquisition blocks, overlapping surveys and differing flight specifications (nominal Area 1 line spacing 200 m; Area 2 400 m). Not every block had a unique aircraft; survey seams require controls.

S16 · Geothermal structural mechanisms

Siler et al. (2019), USGS record ↗ · Open-access article ↗

Structural discontinuities and stress state in two Basin-and-Range geothermal systems. Mechanism context only, not evidence for a specific mapped pixel.

S17 · Great Basin fault-mapping field example

GBCGE Dixie Valley ↗

One candidate scarp was trenched, showed no stratigraphic offset and was interpreted as erosional—a concrete false-positive case, not a rate estimate.

S30 · NBMG Nevada fault-layer scale warning

NBMG MapServer metadata ↗

Regional layer was adapted/modified from NBMG M167 and USGS QFault; metadata states traces were digitized at 1:250,000 and spatial error can be significant when viewed at larger scales. Not a universal error radius for every segment.

Primary scarp-morphology literature

S18 · Hanks et al. (1984)

USGS record ↗ · DOI ↗

Diffusion-style morphology for both wave-cut and fault-controlled landforms; modeled product such as κt is not calendar age without independent diffusivity calibration.

S19 · Andrews & Hanks (1985)

DOI / publisher record ↗

Primary inverse solution for linearly diffusion-degraded scarps; absolute age depends on diffusivity calibration.

S20 · Hilley et al. (2010)

USGS record ↗ · DOI ↗

Second-derivative scarp template, height fit and signal-to-noise applied to scarp-like DEM topography; no tectonic-origin guarantee.

S21 · Sare et al. (2019)

Open-access JGR article ↗

Regional curvature-template workflow reports scarp-like false positives and visual screening for nontectonic features. Not a GeoDAWN validation.
